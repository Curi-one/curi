# Curi — Ideal Customer Profile

> Internal document. This defines who Curi is built for at launch, why that person was chosen, and how to recognise them. Every product decision, every piece of copy, and every GTM action should be tested against this document.

---

## The One-Sentence Version

Curi is built for **technical first-time founders, pre-seed, without a top-tier accelerator network, who have decided to raise and are building their foundational knowledge in real time**.

That is one person. Not a segment, not a demographic. A specific moment in a specific person's life.

---

## Why This Person

The conventional ICP for an edtech product in this space is "curious professionals" or "ambitious self-learners." That was explicitly rejected. The founder ICP was chosen for four reasons.

**The knowledge gap is financially consequential.** A founder who misunderstands their post-money SAFE, misreads their burn multiple, or walks into a term sheet negotiation without knowing what pro-rata rights are will lose real money or real equity. The stakes make the problem a painkiller, not a vitamin.

**The format mismatch is severe.** Every existing solution — MBAs, books, podcasts, courses — requires time a founder in the middle of building does not have. Three minutes a day is not a compromise. It is the only format that fits.

**The community is tight and vocal.** Founders talk to other founders. Indie Hackers, YC forums, founder Twitter, accelerator Slack groups. A product that genuinely helps a founder gets mentioned in exactly these places, unprompted. The distribution is built into the audience.

**The problem is one Awais has personally experienced.** Founder-market fit is real. The insight here is not theoretical — it comes from having been the person who needed this and found nothing adequate.

## The Profile in Full

### Who They Are

A technical founder building their first company. Computer science, engineering, or a hard science background. They understand product and code instinctively. They have almost no formal business training.

They are pre-seed or early seed. They have either raised a small friends-and-family round, are preparing to raise their first institutional round, or are bootstrapping and watching their runway carefully.

They are building outside the top-tier US startup hubs — or they are in a US hub but were not selected by YC, Techstars, or a comparable programme that would otherwise give them access to the structured founder knowledge those networks provide.

They are 25–38 years old. They are time-poor in a particular way: not lazy, but genuinely overloaded. Their days are full of decisions, context-switching, and execution. Sustained reading or structured learning feels inaccessible, not because they lack the intelligence or the desire, but because the cognitive bandwidth is not there by the time the day ends.

### What They Are Doing Right Now

They are trying to raise a pre-seed or seed round. They have had or are preparing for their first investor meetings. They are reading term sheets for the first time, looking up definitions mid-conversation, and aware that they don't fully understand what they are signing.

Or they are pre-fundraise and watching their unit economics for the first time, unsure whether their margins are healthy, what their burn multiple tells a future investor, or how to think about pricing as a function of customer acquisition cost.

In both cases, the learning is happening reactively. They are learning because they have encountered a situation they don't understand, not because they have a structured path to build the knowledge they need before they need it.

### What They Feel

**Overwhelmed.** The volume of things to know is not finite or bounded. There is always another concept, another framework, another thing an investor will assume they understand.

**Aware of the cost.** They know that knowledge gaps are not just uncomfortable — they are expensive. A poorly negotiated SAFE is dilution they will carry for years. A misunderstood burn multiple is a fundraise that fails.

**Too busy for the available options.** They have started Zero to One, Venture Deals, maybe a Coursera course. They have not finished any of them. They listen to podcasts on runs and find them inspiring but retain almost nothing that changes their behaviour.

**Looking for a path, not more content.** They do not want another resource to add to the pile. They want someone to tell them what to learn, in what order, and to make sure it sticks. They want the structure that the YC batch gives to YC founders — but without the selection process or the geography.

### What They Are Not

Not a repeat founder. Not an MBA. Not a growth-stage operator. Not someone who already has the mental models and is looking for inspiration or community. This profile is specific to the first encounter with the foundational knowledge of running a company. Once that knowledge is in place, the product serves a different need.

## The Thiel Refinement

The original ICP was "first-time founders at the 0-to-1 stage." That is correct in direction but too broad as a targeting instrument. Applying a tighter lens:

**Stage specificity.** Not all 0-to-1 founders have the same knowledge gaps at the same time. The highest-value moment is the founder who has decided to raise but has not yet started the process. The knowledge gaps at this moment are specific, urgent, and financially consequential. This is the sharpest version of the ICP.

**The access qualifier.** The insight that separates Curi from every other resource is this: YC founders get this knowledge from their batch. Top accelerator alumni get it from their networks. The Curi ICP is specifically the founder who does not have that network. Not any first-time founder — the one for whom Curi is the only legitimate path to structured foundational knowledge.

**The beachhead community.** Broad targeting across all first-time founders is a broadcast strategy. The Thiel approach is to find a community small enough to achieve 70-80% penetration before expanding. Practically, this means: founders coming out of non-YC accelerators (Entrepreneur First, Seedcamp, SFC, regional programmes), or founders active in Indie Hackers, or founders in geographies where the YC network is thin (UK, Europe, Southeast Asia, ANZ). Own one of these groups completely before expanding to the next.

## What the ICP Is Not

It is worth being precise about the edges of this profile.

| Not the ICP | Why |
|---|---|
| Repeat founders | Already have the foundational knowledge. Different need. |
| MBA graduates joining startups | Have the business training. Miss the practical founder context. |
| Growth-stage operators | Past the 0-to-1 moment. Different knowledge gaps. |
| Curious professionals (broad) | Right interest, wrong urgency. Vitamin, not painkiller. |
| Students studying entrepreneurship | No live stakes. The knowledge does not compound the same way without a real company. |
| YC/top accelerator founders | Have access to the network equivalent. Curi is less differentiated for them. |

## How to Recognise Them

In the wild, this person can be identified by a small number of signals.

They are posting in Indie Hackers about their raise, their burn rate, or their first investor meeting. They are asking questions in founder Slack groups that reveal they are learning the foundational concepts for the first time ("is this SAFE structure normal?", "how do I calculate if my margins are healthy?"). They self-describe as a technical founder who wishes they had more business knowledge. They have a LinkedIn that shows an engineering or product background with no business school and no prior startup exit.

They are not hard to find. They are highly concentrated in a small number of communities and they are vocal about the problems they are experiencing.

## The ICP in One Paragraph

Curi's launch ICP is a technical first-time founder, typically 25–38, who is building their first company outside the YC/top-accelerator network, and is at the moment of preparing to raise or actively navigating their first institutional fundraise. They understand product and code deeply. They have almost no formal business training. They know that their knowledge gaps are costing them — in equity, in negotiating power, in the quality of the decisions they make every day — and they have found every existing learning option either too slow, too generic, or too inaccessible to use consistently. They do not need more content. They need a path, delivered daily, in the time that already exists between everything else.

---

## ICP Evolution

This profile is the launch ICP. It is not permanent.

| Phase | ICP | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | Technical first-time founders, pre-seed, outside top accelerator networks | Acute need, tight community, financially consequential knowledge gap, founder-market fit |
| **Year 2** | Operators and career changers in high-knowledge fields | Same infrastructure, adjacent knowledge domains, broader TAM |
| **Year 3+** | Anyone learning anything | The original mission — every domain, every person, three minutes a day |

The founder niche is not the destination. It is the proof point. The infrastructure built to serve founders — the expert aggregation pipeline, the editorial standards, the progressive curriculum architecture, the daily habit loop — transfers to every knowledge domain that follows. The founders are the books. The mission is the library.
