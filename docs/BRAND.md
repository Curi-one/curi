## 1. Brand Foundations

### 1.1 What Curi Is

Curi is a daily micro-learning system. It takes any topic a person is curious about and constructs a personalised, progressive curriculum — delivered one lesson per morning, three minutes, every day, until the subject belongs to them.

Curi is not a course platform. Not a podcast. Not a newsletter. Not an AI chatbot. It is a new category — the closest analogy is a personal museum of knowledge, curated around a single visitor, open every morning.

### 1.2 Mission

**Rewire how humanity learns.**

The current model is broken. Formal education is slow and inaccessible. Self-directed learning has a 90% abandonment rate. Knowledge should not require a university, three hours, or a Netflix-length attention span. Curi makes continuous learning as automatic as brushing your teeth — and ten times more rewarding.

### 1.3 Brand Positioning

> For **intellectually curious professionals** who want to feel smarter without the overhead of formal learning — Curi is the **only daily learning system** that delivers a structured, expert-verified curriculum on any subject in three minutes a day — so knowledge compounds quietly, permanently, every morning.

### 1.4 Brand Character

Curi occupies the intersection of three worlds:

- **The library** — the weight and authority of accumulated knowledge; the smell of old paper; the quiet of serious reading
- **The contemporary art museum** — precision, white space, editorial confidence; the feeling that curation is itself an art form
- **The technology product** — responsive, personalised, frictionless; built for the way people actually live now

### 1.5 Personality Traits

| Trait | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Scholarly** | Substantive, not superficial. Earns authority rather than claiming it. |
| **Confident** | Never hedges its aesthetic. Never apologises for being intelligent. |
| **Warm** | Not cold. Not corporate. Genuinely interested in the person learning. |
| **Economical** | Every word, every pixel has a reason. Nothing decorative that isn't also structural. |
| **Precise** | Exact language. Exact type sizing. Exact use of colour. No approximations. |
| **Surprising** | Not what you expect from a learning app. Better. |

### 1.6 The One Thing to Remember

**Curi makes people feel genuinely, pleasurably smart.** Every design and copy decision either reinforces that feeling or undermines it. There is no neutral.

## 2. Voice & Tone

### 2.1 The Voice

Curi has one voice, applied consistently across every touchpoint. It is the voice of an exceptionally well-read, intellectually curious friend — someone who knows a great deal but never makes you feel less for not knowing it. A Montaigne, not a professor. An essayist, not a textbook.

**The voice is:** direct without being blunt, intelligent without being inaccessible, warm without being sentimental, confident without being arrogant.

**The voice is never:** breathless, hype-driven, sycophantic, vague, or casual to the point of sloppiness.

### 2.2 Tone by Context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing copy | Composed, quietly confident | *"Three minutes a day. Any subject. Every morning."* |
| Onboarding | Warm and direct | *"Your curriculum is ready. It was built for you."* |
| Lesson content | Scholarly and engaging | *"The Stoics didn't fear death. They used it."* |
| Streak milestones | Celebratory but not gushing | *"Seven days. That's genuine momentum."* |
| Error states | Calm, no drama | *"Something went wrong. Your progress is safe."* |
| Cancellation | Honest and graceful | *"You're always welcome back. Your track will be here."* |

### 2.3 Sentence Construction Rules

- Lead with the insight, not the setup.
- Active voice always. *"Curi delivers a lesson"* not *"A lesson is delivered by Curi."*
- No exclamation marks. Ever.
- No em-dashes. Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop.
- No throat-clearing. Never begin with *"Welcome to..."* or *"We're excited to..."*
- Contractions are fine. Natural, not sloppy.
- Short sentences carry weight. Long sentences carry nuance. Mix deliberately.

### 2.4 Headline Modes

**The Statement** — declarative, no verb required.
> *"Knowledge that compounds."*

**The Paradox** — tension that demands resolution.
> *"The Stoics thought about death every morning. It made them more alive."*

**The Invitation** — framed as a beginning, not a command.
> *"What do you want to understand?"*

## 3. Logo & Wordmark

### 3.1 The Wordmark

The primary Curi identity is a typographic wordmark: **Cu*ri*** — set in Fraunces Light, with the final two characters in italic.

```
Cu·ri
└──┘└┘
 roman italic
```

The italic *ri* creates a subtle typographic inflection — the word leaning forward, curious. It echoes *curio* at the character level. The wordmark is always lettercase as shown. Never ALL CAPS. Never all lowercase. Never redrawn or substituted.

### 3.2 The Vermilion Underline

The wordmark carries a single 3–4px rule beneath it in Vermilion (`#C1121F`). This is the accent — the one drop of colour in the identity. The underline is always present in the primary wordmark. May be omitted in true single-colour contexts (embossed, debossed).

### 3.3 Wordmark Variants

| Variant | Use case | Underline |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** — Ink on White | All standard applications | Vermilion |
| **Reversed** — White on Ink | Dark backgrounds, email footer | Vermilion |
| **Single-colour Ink** | Print, embossing | Absent |
| **Single-colour White** | Reversed single-colour | Absent |

### 3.4 Clear Space

Minimum clear space on all four sides equals the cap-height of *C* in the wordmark.

```
  ↕ cap-height
←→Cu·ri←→
  ↕ cap-height
```

### 3.5 Minimum Sizes

| Medium | Minimum width |
|---|---|
| Digital (screen) | 80px |
| Print | 18mm |
| Physical / embroidery | 25mm |

### 3.6 Incorrect Usage

Never: stretch, compress, or rotate the wordmark. Never apply shadows, glows, gradients, or outlines. Never change the typeface. Never italicise the entire wordmark — the italic is on *ri* only.

## 4. Colour System

### 4.1 The Philosophy

Curi's colour system is warm greyscale with a single, disciplined accent. Eight tones carry intentional warmth — never pure black (`#000000`) or pure white (`#FFFFFF`). Vermilion is the only chromatic accent. Colour is not decoration; it is meaning. When vermilion appears, it is because something matters.

Most products compete on colour. Curi competes on contrast.

### 4.2 The Full Palette

#### Greyscale — Eight Tones

| Token | Name | Hex | RGB | Primary role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `color.ink` | Ink | `#0A0908` | 10, 9, 8 | Primary text, dark backgrounds |
| `color.ink-2` | Ink 2 | `#1C1A18` | 28, 26, 24 | Hover on dark surfaces |
| `color.ink-3` | Ink 3 | `#2E2C28` | 46, 44, 40 | Secondary dark surfaces |
| `color.mid` | Mid | `#6B6760` | 107, 103, 96 | Secondary text, supporting copy |
| `color.silver` | Silver | `#9E9B94` | 158, 155, 148 | Labels, captions, metadata |
| `color.light` | Light | `#D4D0C8` | 212, 208, 200 | Borders, rules, subtle backgrounds |
| `color.paper` | Paper | `#F4F1E8` | 244, 241, 232 | Secondary background, cards |
| `color.white` | White | `#FAF9F5` | 250, 249, 245 | Primary background |

Never use pure black (`#000000`) or pure white (`#FFFFFF`) — the warmth is intentional. Vermilion is the only chromatic value in the entire palette.

#### The Accent

| Token | Name | Hex | RGB | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `color.accent` | Vermilion | `#C1121F` | 193, 18, 31 | Single accent — used once per screen |

### 4.3 The Vermilion Rule

Vermilion appears **once per screen, per layout, per composition.** Permitted uses:

1. The wordmark underline
2. The pull-quote left border rule in lesson content
3. The CTA button bottom underline
4. The image-hover reveal line
5. Numbered list indicators in lesson takeaways
6. The cursor tracking dot in interactive contexts
7. The Vermilion accent card in exhibition grids (background fill — used once per page maximum)

**Never:** body text, decorative use, more than once per layout, in combination with any non-neutral colour. Never pair Vermilion on Paper (fails contrast).

The test: *is this the most important thing on this screen?* If not, solve the problem another way.

### 4.4 Approved Colour Pairings

| Foreground | Background | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | White | Primary body text |
| Ink | Paper | Cards, secondary surfaces |
| White | Ink | Hero panels, footer, marquee |
| Silver | Ink | Metadata on dark |
| Ink | Light | Subtle differentiation |
| Vermilion | Ink | Accent on dark |
| Vermilion | White | Accent on light |

**Never pair:** Vermilion on Paper (fails contrast), Silver on White for body text (it is for labels and metadata only), or any pairing that fails WCAG AA.

### 4.5 Accessibility

All text must meet WCAG 2.1 AA:
- Body text: 4.5:1 minimum
- Large text (18px+ or 14px+ bold): 3:1 minimum
- UI components and graphics: 3:1 minimum

## 5. Typography

### 5.1 The System

Curi uses three typefaces. The pairing principle: a distinctive, slightly peculiar display serif that gives headlines character and authority; a clean humanist sans that disappears as UI chrome; a monospace for metadata and technical labels. The serif earns attention at scale; the sans earns trust in use.

### 5.2 Display — Fraunces

**Use for:** wordmark, lesson titles, section headlines, card titles, pull quotes at display scale, stat numbers used as design elements.

Fraunces (by Undercase Type) is a variable "wonky" serif — an optical typeface with deliberate eccentricities that feel intentional and bookish without being old-fashioned. Its thick-thin contrast is more restrained than Cormorant, making it viable at slightly smaller display sizes. The italic is characterful and warm.

**Why Fraunces for Curi:** it sits at the exact intersection of scholarly and contemporary — a typeface that would be at home in a serious art book or a well-designed product. It does not look like any other learning app.

**Axes (variable font):**
- `wght`: 100–900 — use 300 (Light) for display headlines, 400 for card titles
- `SOFT`: 0–100 — set to 50–100 for headlines; lower values are sharper
- `WONK`: 0–1 — set to 1 to enable optical quirks; 0 for more conventional forms

**Recommended settings by context:**

| Context | Weight | SOFT | WONK | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | 300 | 80 | 1 | 72–192px |
| Section headline | 300 | 60 | 1 | 48–72px |
| Lesson title | 400 | 50 | 1 | 32–52px |
| Card title | 400 | 40 | 0 | 20–28px |
| Pull quote display | 300 italic | 80 | 1 | 28–36px |

**Letter-spacing:**
- 80px+: `-0.04em`
- 48–80px: `-0.03em`
- 32–48px: `-0.02em`
- 20–32px: `-0.015em`

**Never use Fraunces below 18px.** Below this threshold, use Plus Jakarta Sans.

### 5.3 UI & Body — Plus Jakarta Sans

**Use for:** all UI chrome, buttons, navigation labels, form inputs, body copy in non-lesson contexts, badges, tooltips, system messages, onboarding copy, email body.

Plus Jakarta Sans (by Tokotype) is a humanist geometric sans with warm proportions. Its slightly curved geometry prevents it from feeling cold, while its precision makes it legible at 10px. It holds no opinion at small sizes — which is exactly what a UI sans should do.

**Weights used:**
- Light (300) — body copy, supporting text, descriptions
- Regular (400) — standard UI labels, navigation, input text
- Medium (500) — buttons, active states, emphasis
- SemiBold (600) — strong emphasis, prominent labels
- Light Italic — editorial body, lesson subtext in non-serif contexts

**Size range:** 10px minimum (labels) to 18px (featured body). Above 18px, prefer Fraunces.

**Line height:**
- 10–12px labels: 1.3–1.4
- 13–15px body: 1.6–1.7
- 16–18px featured body: 1.7–1.75

**Letter-spacing:**
- Standard: `0` (no adjustment needed at body sizes)
- Uppercase labels: `0.12em` to `0.2em`
- Buttons: `0.06em` to `0.1em`

### 5.4 Metadata — JetBrains Mono

**Use for:** lesson day/number indicators (Day 7 of 14), streak counts, timestamps, hex values, metadata strings, system labels, code contexts, progress fraction displays.

JetBrains Mono provides the technical precision needed for metadata. Its slightly condensed proportions and generous x-height make it legible at very small sizes — 8px is viable for the smallest labels.

**Weights used:** Light (300), Regular (400).

**Letter-spacing:** `0.15em` to `0.25em` for uppercase labels. `0` for numerals used standalone.

**Size range:** 8–11px. Never used at display sizes.

### 5.6 Typographic Rules

**Hierarchy through scale and weight, not colour.** Body copy is always Ink or Mid. Never change text to Vermilion for emphasis — use italic or a size step.

**Widows and orphans.** Never allow a single word alone on the final line of a headline. Adjust manually.

**Smart quotes always.** Never straight quotes (`"` `'`). Always typographic (`"` `"` `'` `'`).

**No type effects.** No shadows, outlines, gradients, glows. No faux bold. No faux italic.

**Hyphenation in headlines: never.** Rewrite the line.

**Fraunces + Plus Jakarta Sans never appear at the same size.** If both are present in a component, there must be a clear size or weight distinction. The serif leads; the sans supports.

## 6. Imagery & Visual Language

### 6.1 The Approach

Curi uses no photography of people. No stock imagery. No AI-generated illustration. The visual language is constructed entirely from typography, geometry, and texture — the raw material of knowledge itself. Photographs insert demographic signals into a brand that should feel universal. Curi's imagery should feel like it could only have been made for Curi.

### 6.2 Typographic Imagery

The primary image format is large typographic forms — glyphs, letterforms, mathematical symbols, and typographic ornaments — rendered at enormous scale in Fraunces Italic, on dark field.

**Approved typographic image elements:**

| Glyph | Name | Domain association |
|---|---|---|
| `§` | Section mark | Law, structure, annotation |
| `∮` | Contour integral | Mathematics, completeness |
| `Æ` | Ligature | History, language, connection |
| `∞` | Infinity | Mathematics, philosophy |
| `I` | Roman numeral | History, time, classical |
| `ℵ` | Aleph | Mathematics, language, infinity |
| `¶` | Pilcrow | Writing, editing, annotation |
| `†` | Dagger | Reference, footnote |
| `∴` | Therefore | Logic, reasoning |
| `∵` | Because | Logic, causation |
| `⊕` | Circled plus | Science, combination |

The symbol should connect to the subject matter of the content it accompanies. Never use a symbol purely decoratively.

**Construction standard:**
- Glyph at 40–60% opacity, slightly off-centre
- Dark field: two-stop gradient, Ink to Ink 2
- Halftone dot overlay at 3–5% opacity
- Diagonal light band at 2–3% opacity
- Fine grain texture at 2–4% opacity

### 6.3 Geometric Forms

Secondary imagery draws on the architectural vocabulary of museums and libraries: ruled lines, grid structures, diagonal hatching, precise geometry. Always constructed in greyscale. Never in colour.

### 6.4 The Vermilion Reveal Line

The single permitted use of colour in imagery: a 2–4px Vermilion rule appearing at the bottom or edge of an image panel on hover, or as a static element in print. Never thicker than 4px. A thread, not a flag.

### 6.5 Image Aspect Ratios

| Context | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Exhibition grid card | 3:4 |
| Email header | 16:5 |
| Social card | 1:1 |
| LinkedIn article header | 16:9 |
| Print / certificate | A4 or Letter |

## 7. Motion & Animation

### 7.1 Principles

Motion at Curi should feel like pages turning, not like a product announcement. Inevitable, not surprising. Slow where possible. Purposeful at all times.

Three motion principles: **Gravity** (things settle into place, never fly in), **Patience** (300ms minimum for meaningful transitions), **Restraint** (one motion at a time).

No spring physics. No bounce. No elastic. No parallax scrolling effects.

### 7.2 Easing Functions

```css
/* Primary — settling in */
--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1);

/* Exit — leaving */
--ease-in: cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1);

/* Standard — both directions */
--ease-standard: cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1);
```

### 7.3 Duration Scale

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `duration.micro` | 80–120ms | Hover fills, focus rings |
| `duration.small` | 180–220ms | Colour and opacity transitions |
| `duration.medium` | 300–400ms | Component entrances, modals |
| `duration.large` | 600–900ms | Page transitions, staggered reveals |
| `duration.xl` | 1000–1200ms | Wordmark draw-in, hero entrances |

### 7.4 Standard Transitions

**Wordmark underline draw-in:**
```css
transform: scaleX(0) → scaleX(1);
transform-origin: left;
duration: 1100ms;
easing: var(--ease-out);
delay: 700ms after page load;
```

**Content reveal (fade up):**
```css
opacity: 0 → 1;
transform: translateY(20px) → translateY(0);
duration: 900ms;
easing: var(--ease-out);
stagger: 130ms between sequential elements;
```

**Image hover Vermilion line:**
```css
transform: scaleX(0) → scaleX(1);
transform-origin: left;
duration: 350ms;
easing: var(--ease-out);
trigger: parent :hover;
```

### 7.5 Reduced Motion

All animations must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`:
```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  * {
    animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    transition-duration: 0.01ms !important;
  }
}
```

Design tokens are the single source of truth for all visual values. They must be defined before any component is built. Any value not defined in tokens should not exist in the product.

### 8.3 Spacing Tokens

All spacing derives from a base-4 scale. The primary rhythm is multiples of 8.

```css
--space-1:    4px;    /* Micro — inline gaps */
--space-2:    8px;    /* XSmall — tight component padding */
--space-3:   12px;    /* Small-tight */
--space-4:   16px;    /* Small — component internal padding */
--space-5:   20px;    /* Small-medium */
--space-6:   24px;    /* Medium — between related elements */
--space-8:   32px;    /* Large — between components */
--space-10:  40px;    /* XLarge — section padding */
--space-12:  48px;    /* 2XL — section internal */
```

### 8.4 Border Radius Tokens

Curi uses minimal border-radius. The default is zero (sharp corners). Radius is used only in specific, justified contexts.

```css
--radius-none:   0px;     /* Default — all containers, cards, buttons */
--radius-sm:     2px;     /* Badge pills — only when content is very short */
--radius-md:     4px;     /* Internal chip elements only */
--radius-full: 9999px;    /* Circular elements only (avatar initials) */
```

**Rule:** If you are tempted to add border-radius to a container, card, or button, the default answer is no. Sharp corners are the brand. Exceptions must be explicitly justified.

### 8.5 Shadow Tokens

Curi uses no box shadows on cards or containers. Depth is achieved through background colour differentiation and border rules.

```css
--shadow-none: none;    /* Default — all containers */
```

The one permitted use of visual depth: a 2px bottom border in Vermilion on the active CTA button. This is a border, not a shadow.

### 8.6 Border Tokens

## 13. Marketing & Acquisition

### 13.1 The Homepage

The homepage is the product, not a description of the product. Its single job: deliver a *"this was made for me"* moment before asking for an account.

**Above the fold:** one input field, one headline. No navigation initially visible. The input is the CTA.

> *"What do you want to understand?"*

**Below the fold:** scrolling exhibition grid of real curriculum examples. Specific, compelling lesson titles. No testimonials at launch. No feature grid. No pricing table.

### 13.2 Advertising Principles

- Lead with the insight, not the product
- Show a real lesson title in the first frame
- No urgency manufacture, no discount mechanics
- Twitter/X and LinkedIn only — typographic cards
- Black background, Fraunces display type, wordmark bottom-right, Vermilion underline

### 13.3 Referral and Sharing

**Lesson share card:** insight first, brand second. `"I just learned: [insight] — Curi, Day [N]"`. Black background, White text. No Vermilion (too loud for social context).

**Track completion card:** certificate style. White, centred, Fraunces. Vermilion rule between subject name and wordmark — the certificate earns the accent.

## 16. Brand Don'ts

### 16.1 Colour Don'ts

- Never add a second accent colour. If you need one, the layout is the problem.
- Never use Vermilion for success states. Correct = Ink fill. Not green. Not Vermilion.
- Never use pure `#000000` or pure `#FFFFFF`. Warm greyscale *is* the brand — use the eight defined tones only.
- Never introduce a second chromatic colour outside Vermilion.
- Never use gradients on type.
- Never use Vermilion more than once per screen.
- Never pair Vermilion on Paper (fails contrast).

### 16.2 Typography Don'ts

- Never use system fonts in designed contexts.
- Never use all-caps for display headlines.
- Never mix typefaces outside the three-font system.
- Never apply faux bold or faux italic.
- Never use type effects: shadows, outlines, glows, strokes.
- Never use Fraunces below 18px.

### 16.3 Layout Don'ts

- Never centre-align standard layouts. Centre is for certificates and completion screens only.
- Never use drop shadows on cards or containers.
- Never use border-radius on containers or buttons (default: 0).
- Never use dotted grids or pattern fills as backgrounds.

### 16.4 Imagery Don'ts

- Never use stock photography.
- Never use AI-generated illustration.
- Never use photographs of people.
- Never use colour photography.
- Never add illustration to compensate for weak writing.

### 16.5 Voice Don'ts

- Never use exclamation marks.
- Never use em-dashes.
- Never write for the brand. Write for the reader.
- Never motivate through anxiety. Curi motivates through the pleasure of learning.
- Never be vague to seem sophisticated.

## 17. Design Principles

Twelve principles that govern every decision. When you are unsure, return here.

---

**01 — The content is the design.**
Everything exists to deliver the lesson, the quiz, the feel-smart moment. If an element is not serving that delivery, remove it.

---

**02 — Restraint is a design decision, not a limitation.**
The warm greyscale palette, the single accent, the three-font system — these are choices. They communicate discipline and authority. Every time you want to add a second accent or a fourth typeface, you are proposing a different brand.

---

**03 — Vermilion earns its appearance.**
The accent is powerful because it is rare. Every additional use dilutes all previous uses. Treat each use as a transaction: *is this the most important thing on this screen?*

---

**04 — Tokens before components, components before layouts.**
Nothing is designed outside the token system. A hardcoded hex value in a component is a bug. A component that does not use tokens is technical debt.

---

**05 — Typography is the primary visual medium.**
No illustrations. No icons. No mascots. Type is how Curi creates atmosphere, hierarchy, beauty, and meaning. Invest in it before anything else.

---

**06 — People are the protagonist.**
Not *"Curi's lessons"* — *"your curriculum."* Not *"our platform"* — *"the track you built."* The design should reinforce this constantly.

---

**07 — Density is earned.**
Empty space is the default. Fill it only when content has sufficient weight to deserve it.

---

**08 — Never surprise negatively.**
Motion should feel inevitable. Interactions should have no unexpected outcomes. The only surprise Curi delivers is the intellectual one.

---

**09 — The brand lives in the details.**
The difference between a well-built Curi screen and a generic learning-app screen is almost entirely in the details: the negative tracking, the paper tone, the width of the rule, the line height. Get the details right.

---

**10 — Quality before completeness.**
Fewer, better things over more, adequate things. A product with three features executed with total precision beats ten features executed adequately.

---

**11 — The habit is the product.**
Every design decision should ask: *does this help this person come back tomorrow?* Nothing else matters more than that.

---

**12 — Leave the door open.**
For cancellation, for missed days, for incomplete tracks — Curi never punishes. The track is always waiting. The brand communicates permanence and patience.
